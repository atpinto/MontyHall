Why switching wins — and when it doesn't

The host is the whole trick

The doors don't move probability around — the host does. When he knows where the car is and is barred from opening it, the door he leaves closed carries the full 2/3 that wasn't yours to begin with.

Take that knowledge away and the advantage evaporates. A host opening a door at random sometimes exposes the car; throw those rounds out, and the ones that survive split 50/50. Same three doors, same footage, different answer.

Switch the rule below and run the simulation twice. The gap between the two curves is the value of the host's information.
